Shopify already ships everything around the phone line.

Shopify Payments turned a software company into a merchant-services company. In Q3 2026, merchant solutions made up 78% of Shopify’s $3.7 billion in revenue.1 Connectivity is the same kind of business, and brands outside telecom are already in it: Klarna,2 Comcast,3 and Wealthsimple, where I helped launch a phone plan with TELUS.4

Shopify already sells a POS handheld with an eSIM inside,5 sends texts through Shopify Messaging6 and has an AI assistant that can hold a voice conversation.7 What’s missing is the phone line underneath them.

The case for Shopify Wireless.

Some of this is revenue Shopify can add in the first year on a partner’s network. The rest only becomes possible once Shopify runs its own core. Each reason is marked with the phase that unlocks it.

Revenue Shopify can add in year one

Connectivity sold the way Shopify already sells Payments: built in, billed with the plan.

Put a Shopify SIM in the hardware Light MVNO

The Victa Mobile has an eSIM and a SIM tray, and Shopify’s offline GMV grew 30% last quarter.8 Ship every device with Shopify connectivity switched on: Wi-Fi first, LTE the moment it drops. Merchants stop losing sales to bad Wi-Fi, pop-ups and market stalls work anywhere, and Shopify earns a monthly fee on every device.

Work lines for every merchant’s team Light MVNO

A store owner adds a work number for each of their employees from the Shopify admin. It installs as an eSIM on the employee’s own phone, next to their personal number, and it’s tied to their POS staff account. The owner can switch lines off after hours and see every customer call. When an employee leaves, the number stays with the store, and so do the customers who have it.

Include a free line, then sell the upgrades Light MVNO

Every Shopify plan comes with a business phone number. The free line gets it adopted; employee work lines, device SIMs and Sidekick for calls are the paid add-ons. Comcast’s free-line offer shows how well this converts: about 70% of its free mobile lines turn into paid ones.9

Products only a carrier can build

When Shopify carries the call, the call becomes part of the commerce platform.

Turn calls into customer data Light, then full

Every call lands on the customer timeline next to orders and texts. Unknown callers become leads, spam never rings, and a wholesale inquiry turns into a B2B company and a draft order. Merchants finally see which campaigns made the phone ring. At launch, every line gets call logs and the business line gets AI summaries; with our own core, AI covers every employee’s line too.

Put Sidekick inside the call Full MVNO

On our own core network, every call can pass through Sidekick. It answers “where’s my order?” from live order data, transcribes and summarizes each conversation, and hands harder calls to a person with the context already on screen. Sales teams get call notes they didn’t have to write.

Reach customers from the store’s own number Full MVNO

Owning the numbers and the network lets Shopify verify the store’s calls, show its name on caller ID, and send RCS messages as a verified business. Sidekick drafts the follow-up, and it goes out from the number customers already know, only to people who opted in.

Why it compounds

Wireless feeds the parts of Shopify that already work.

Every call can end in a checkout

Sidekick or an employee takes the order on the phone and texts a Shop Pay link before the caller hangs up. Calls stop being a cost and become GMV that runs through Shopify Payments.

The phone number is the stickiest thing a merchant owns

It’s on the Google listing, the receipts and the side of the delivery van. Once a store ports its number to Shopify, the phone line is one more reason to stay.

Light first. Full when it’s earned.

A light MVNO gets Shopify to market in months, using a partner’s core on a host network. Once merchants prove the demand, Shopify moves to its own cloud-native core. That’s the step that puts Sidekick inside the call. I’ve run both models, and I helped launch a US MVNO on a cloud-native core at mobi.10

  1. Phase 1, months 0–3

    Deals and design

    Shortlist MVNE and host partners across AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon. Negotiate wholesale rates, numbering and porting. Cover the regulatory basics: 911, customer data rules, taxes and fees. Design the admin surfaces with the POS, Customers and Sidekick teams.

  2. Phase 2, months 3–9

    US launch as a light MVNO

    Shopify-branded SIMs and eSIMs through an MVNE. A business line in every plan, paid work lines for merchants’ employees, and device SIMs for POS hardware. Call logs, voicemail transcription and summaries on the business line.

  3. Phase 3, months 9–18

    Prove it

    Pilot to general availability against clear gates: attach rate, lines per merchant, churn of merchants with lines against those without, number ports and add-on conversion. Then a go or no-go on the full build.

  4. Phase 4, months 18–36

    Full MVNO

    Shopify’s own cloud-native core, its own SIM profiles and more than one host network. Sidekick in the call path, verified calling and RCS business messaging. Canada next, where I’ve already negotiated with every national carrier.

My first 90 days.

The goal by day 90 is a decision: a pilot Shopify can fund, with partners lined up and the merchant case proven.

  1. Days 1–30

    Learn the merchant

    Talk to POS, B2B and online merchants about how they handle phones, employee lines and store connectivity today. Size demand by segment, and map where Phone would meet POS, Customers, Messaging and Sidekick.

  2. Days 31–60

    Get to term sheets

    Run a competitive process across three or four MVNE and host partners. Compare light and full costs over five years, and lay out the regulatory checklist for the US launch.

  3. Days 61–90

    Bring a pilot plan

    A 50-merchant pilot with admin designs, success gates and a launch budget. A business case leadership can say yes or no to.
